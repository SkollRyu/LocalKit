Rule-based observations

Tools

1. Pension contribution optimiser

Pick a goal. The tool computes the extra gross pension contribution needed, its true cost from your take-home pay, and the effective relief rate once tax, NI and Child Benefit are all accounted for.

2. Wrapper allocation solver

Rules-based order for placing new money: ISA headroom first, then pension for higher-rate/taper/Child-Benefit relief, then GIA. Assumes money isn't needed before retirement for anything routed to pension.

Assumptions & simplifications

This tool models England & Northern Ireland income tax only (not Scotland). It is a planning illustration, not tax advice, and it does not connect to HMRC or store any data.

  • Tax code: standard NNNNL codes set the Personal Allowance base; K codes add the equivalent to taxable income; other codes (BR, D0, 0T, NT…) fall back to the standard £12,570 allowance with taper applied, shown as a note.
  • Pension contribution methods are modelled so that, once all reliefs are claimed, total annual tax liability is the same for net pay and relief-at-source — the difference shown is cash-flow (how much leaves your pay this month) and, for salary sacrifice, employee & employer National Insurance savings. Employer contributions are assumed to be calculated on your original contractual salary, not the post-sacrifice salary.
  • GIA / Stocks & Shares ISA growth is treated as unrealised — no Capital Gains Tax is charged until you tell the tool a gain has been realised (the "capital gains realised this year" field), matching how CGT actually works.
  • Per-holding tax for interest and dividends is attributed using your pooled average rate for that income type, since UK tax bands apply progressively to your total income, not per account.
  • Gilts: coupon income is taxed as savings interest; capital gains on individual UK gilts are exempt from CGT, so gilt "growth" (if any) is always shown tax-free.
  • Pension withdrawals are not modelled — growth is shown tax-free while invested and locked until minimum pension age, ignoring the eventual 25% tax-free lump sum / marginal-rate tax on the rest.
  • Not modelled: Scotland, Gift Aid, Marriage Allowance, Blind Person's Allowance, MPAA triggering, defined-benefit accrual, student loans, multiple employments, self-employment (Class 2/4 NI).
