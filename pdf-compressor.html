Smaller PDFs, with the receipts.

Most oversized PDFs are oversized for one reason: images stored at far higher resolution than they are ever drawn at. This tool finds every image, works out its effective DPI on the page, and resamples only the ones above your threshold. Text, vectors and fonts are left untouched — the document stays searchable and selectable. Every decision is listed below so you can check the cost.

What the verdict column will say

Every image object in the file gets one of these. Nothing is silently degraded, and several codecs are deliberately left alone because re-encoding them would cost quality and gain nothing.

resampled
Drawn above the ceiling. Reduced to the target resolution and re-encoded as JPEG.
under ceiling
Already at or below the target. Untouched — re-encoding it would only add generation loss.
bilevel
A one-bit scan in CCITT G4 or JBIG2. JPEG would add ringing around the text and make the file bigger.
masked
Uses colour-key masking or an inverted decode array, where changing the samples would break the transparency.
unsupported
JPEG 2000, LZW or CMYK data the browser cannot decode reliably. Passed through as-is.
no gain
Resampling was tried and came out larger. The original was put back.

Built on pdf-lib for object surgery and the browser's own JPEG encoder, with page content streams parsed to recover each image's placement matrix — that is how effective DPI is measured rather than guessed. Encrypted and password-protected files are declined rather than silently mangled. Large scans are held in memory a page at a time; a tab has a few gigabytes to play with, so extremely large files may still run out.
